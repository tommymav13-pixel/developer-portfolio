# Philosophy

This document describes the principles that guide how I learn, build
software, and develop as a programmer.

---

## Why I Build

I enjoy solving complex problems through structured and analytical thinking.

Software development allows me to combine problem solving, mathematics,
creativity, and continuous learning while building systems that produce
practical results.

---

## Learning Philosophy

I value understanding over memorization.

Whenever possible, I want to understand why something works rather than
only learning the syntax required to make it work.

My learning process is based on:

- understanding concepts;
- applying them through exercises;
- experimenting independently;
- making and analysing mistakes;
- documenting important lessons;
- applying knowledge in projects.

Consistency is more valuable than occasional intensive effort.

---

## Development Principles

- Build consistently.
- Learn by doing.
- Understand before memorizing.
- Break complex problems into smaller parts.
- Prefer clarity over cleverness.
- Use conventions consistently.
- Document knowledge that is worth reusing.
- Review and improve previous work.
- Finish meaningful projects.

---

## Problem Solving

I prefer to approach technical problems systematically.

A useful general process is:

```text
Understand the problem
        ↓
Break it into smaller components
        ↓
Identify assumptions and constraints
        ↓
Develop a solution
        ↓
Test the result
        ↓
Investigate failures
        ↓
Improve the solution
